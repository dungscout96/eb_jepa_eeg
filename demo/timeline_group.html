How to read this

  • Only the query changes. The candidate pool is the same 101 V-JEPA-2 moments throughout, so chance stays 1.0% at Top-1 and every K is directly comparable.
  • What it buys. Top-10 goes 18% at K=1 to 63% at K=106; Top-1 rises too but flattens around K≈32. Averaging brains recovers the stimulus-locked component; averaging seconds within one brain does not — see snr_scaling §2.6.
  • Read the small-K rows with the error bars. There are only 101 queries, so a single set of K participants carries ~3 points of binomial noise at Top-1. Each tab also reports the mean ± sd over 40 random sets of that size; the K=106 tab is every live participant, so it is a single set by construction.
  • The trace is the same average. Mean |z| falls from 0.30 at K=1 to 0.08 at K=106 — subject-specific activity cancels, the shared response does not. That ratio implies a single-trial reliability of roughly 0.06, in the range measured by CorrCA in snr_scaling §2.
  • Dead recordings are excluded (2 of 108 on this split carry no usable EEG). Single-participant behaviour, including who is strong and who is not, is on the per-participant page.
