How to read this

  • Three participants, not one. Per-participant Top-1 on this split ranges from 0.000 to 0.178 across 108 people, median 0.030, and the differences are real — split-half reliability r = 0.81. The "strongest" tab is the best of 108, so its number is inflated by that selection (its own two halves give 0.216 and 0.140). "Typical" and "weakest" are there so the page cannot be read as the method's typical behaviour.
  • Time level is the hardest granularity — 101 candidates, one per 2 s of film, chance Top-1 1.0%. Shot and scene level are easier; see the scene-level page.
  • The query is a single 2 s window encoded on its own — no temporal context, no averaging across participants. Averaging across participants helps a lot; see the subject-averaged page.
  • The ribbon shows where in the film the model is right: bar height and opacity encode the rank of the correct moment on a log scale (rank 1 = full height). Long dark runs are stretches it tracks; pale runs are stretches it loses.
  • Traces are per-recording z-scored, drawn at 50 Hz and clipped at ±4 SD for display. Bars under each candidate are cosine similarity on a shared scale; only the ordering is used.
